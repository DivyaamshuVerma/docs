**Different Logging Levels:**



Logging levels are used to categorize and prioritize log messages based on their severity or significance. The specific set of logging levels can vary depending on the logging framework or library being used, but the following are the commonly used logging levels:



|Logging Level|Description|
|-|-|
|Fatal|This is the most severe logging level. It is used to log critical errors that result in the termination or shutdown of the application. Fatal logs indicate catastrophic failures that require immediate attention.|
|Error|This level is used to log errors or exceptional conditions that prevent the application or a specific operation from functioning properly. Error logs indicate significant issues that need to be addressed.|
|Warn|This level indicates potential issues or situations that could lead to problems in the future. It signifies events or conditions that may not be errors but require attention or investigation.|
|Info|This level is used for general informational messages that highlight the progress or notable events in the application. Info logs provide high-level insights into the application's functioning.|
|Debug|The debug level is used for logging detailed information that is primarily useful for debugging purposes. Debug logs provide insights into the inner workings of the application and help developers identify and fix problems during development or testing.|
|Trace|The lowest and most detailed logging level. It is used for highly detailed and fine-grained information about the application's execution flow. Trace logs are typically used for debugging and troubleshooting specific issues.|
|All|This level captures all types of logs, including the standard logging levels mentioned above as well as any custom logs implemented in the application.|
|Off|This level disables all logging. No log messages will be generated when the logging level is set to OFF.|
